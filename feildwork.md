## 2. What I Observed

I visited Jhelum Mess to understand how rotis are prepared and managed during lunch and dinner.

I wanted to know how the staff decides the number of rotis to prepare for each meal, especially since the number of students eating can vary from day to day.

Preparing fewer rotis than required can create problems for students, while preparing too many can lead to unnecessary leftovers and food wastage.

This made me interested in whether keeping a record of daily roti preparation and consumption could help the staff make better estimates.

## 3. Conversation with the Mess Staff

I spoke with Abdul Qayoom (Butler) and Mohammad Mukhtar (Cook) to understand how the mess handles its daily roti requirements.

**Things discussed:**

1. I wanted to understand how the staff currently estimates the number of rotis required for lunch and dinner.
2. We discussed how student attendance and daily variations in the number of students can affect roti requirements.
3. I also wanted to understand what happens when rotis are either insufficient or left over after a meal.

**Something that surprised me:**

I initially thought that the problem was mainly about preparing too many or too few rotis. However, I realised that the more important challenge is estimating the actual requirement before each meal, particularly when student attendance is unpredictable.

## 5. What I Learned
